- **Gunners:** every crew-served gun has its gunner now: a technical's DShK or .50, a Humvee's .50 in its turret, the light strike vehicles' pintle guns, the armed motorboat's PKM and a Ghost Hawk's two door guns. He stands behind the gun (kneels at a boat's bow, crouches in a helicopter's door) with his hands on the grips and turns with it as it's laid. Remote weapon stations and turret guns are worked from inside, as before.
- **Door guns** rest pointing out of their doors, the gunner inside behind them, and swing out to what they shoot at.
- **Up close:** the guns are phosphated steel, near black and matte, worn where hands and belts rub them. The .50 has the cooling holes through its barrel support, its trunnion block, rivets and a flaring flash hider; the 7.62s a slotted flash hider and front sight.
- **Barrel smoke:** a gun hot from a burst goes on smoking when it stops, thin wisps curling off the muzzle and drifting away, thicker after a long burst and from a cannon.
