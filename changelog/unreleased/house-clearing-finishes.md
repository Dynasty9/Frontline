title = "Houses get cleared to the end"
summary = "Men who storm a building now call it clear once they've looked round, a clearing cut short is tried again (through a blown-out wall if the doors are gone), and a man pressed into a corner by a wall no longer freezes there."
kind = "fixed"
+++

- **The call:** whoever goes in calls "Clear!" if nobody's left on his feet inside, whether he went in after a man
  seen in there or just to check it. An enemy he can see out of a window, running off across the field, no longer
  stops him.
- **Clearing again:** if the pair sent to clear a building never gets in (the point man hit at the door, or called
  away to drag in a wounded mate), the squad doesn't leave it unchecked for ten minutes: another pair goes, or if
  its men are inside by then, one of them looks round and calls it.
- **Through the gap:** a stretch of wall blown down to rubble is a way in as good as a door. A house whose doors
  were both blown out in the fight used to be left uncleared.
- **Stuck by a wall:** a soldier thrown into the corner of a wall by a blast, or standing tight against one at a
  window, could be sent the long way round from the far side of it and stand frozen trying to take the first step.
  He now sets off on his own side. In one of twelve test battles for the parish church, a fighter stood frozen in
  the chancel arch to the end.
