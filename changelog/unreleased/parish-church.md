- **The church** stands back from one of the roads into the town, its tower to the road: a nave of coursed stone with dressed quoins and buttresses, tall traceried windows with a hint of stained glass, a lower chancel, a steep slate roof, and a west tower with a clock, louvred belfry, battlements, pinnacles and a slate spire with a cross.
- **Two ways in**: the west door, through the open archway at the foot of the tower, and the south door in a timber porch on low stone walls (cover for whoever holds it). Both open like a house's door, and men clearing the church stack on both.
- **A place to fight**: the windows are chest high to fire from, the roofs lift off when your men are inside, over the pews, the pulpit, the lectern, the font, the choir stalls and the altar, and a squad told to hold the church takes its places inside, at the windows and doors. Its slate roof rarely catches fire; its walls give cover and come down to rubble like a house's.
- **The churchyard** round it has a low stone wall with a gate, paths up to the west door and round to the porch, rows of graves either side and behind, and a dark conifer at each front corner.
- Missions' towns are unchanged; a skirmish map with no room by its roads has no church.

- A broader path through the gate and a small west-door landing make the entrance readable from command height. The linked path to the south porch is wider too.
