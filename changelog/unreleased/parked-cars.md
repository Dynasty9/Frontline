- **Parking lanes**: the two main streets through the middle of town are wider than the side streets, with a parking lane down each side. A solid line marks it off from the two traffic lanes, and the dashed centre line runs between them. The pavements, lamps and houses along them stand further back to make room. Side streets have no room to park.
- **Parking bays** are marked out in the parking lanes in a few short runs by the shops, and about half of them have a car in. Every other car in town stands in a driveway or a car park, not in the street.
- **Left where they were parked**: most face the way the traffic ran on their side of the street, and now and then one has burnt out in its bay.
- **Where nobody parks**: no bay is painted in a junction's mouth, on a zebra, across a driveway or by a hydrant. Both traffic lanes stay open past the parked cars, so a lorry or a tank still gets down the street.
- **For the fighting**: riflemen take cover behind the cars, and they catch fire and burn like the cars in the drives.
- Parking bays are in battles only: the missions' streets have their parking lanes but no parked cars.
