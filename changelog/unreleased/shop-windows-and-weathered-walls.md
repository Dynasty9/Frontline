- **Shops** have display windows across their fronts, under the fascia: a painted stall riser and pilasters, a slim
  frame with a transom light, and boxes, tins and jars set out behind the glass. Some panes are taped or shot out,
  and some shops are lit at night. The joinery and the fascia over it share one paint, in the bottle green, navy,
  oxblood, charcoal and teal that shopfronts are painted.
- **Walls** gather grime under the eaves, where the overhang keeps the rain off, and are damp just above the
  plinth. Here and there a dark run comes down from a gutter. This covers the storeys over the ground floor too.
- The joints between the panels a wall was built from no longer show as lines every few metres, so a rendered
  wall reads as one surface.
- House windows by day are old glass catching a little of the sky, not black holes.
- Men fight from a shop window as from any other, and its riser is cover for a man kneeling.
