- **Front gardens** with room for it have a bed of flowers, or a row of shrubs of one kind: box balls, hydrangeas with big round heads of flowers, roses or lavender. They're kept clear of the door, the bins and the pots.
- **Flower beds** are planted in rows the way a gardener does it: spires of flowers at the back, cushions of flowers in the middle and a low white edging in front, in two or three colours.
- **Clipped hedges** run along the front of nearly half the unfenced gardens, shorn flat and lighter on top, with gaps for the path, the drive and the mailbox. The hedges between the back gardens are kept clipped too. They still give the same cover.
- **Vegetable plots** have rows of cabbages, lettuces, potatoes and carrots, and on most plots runner beans up crossed canes.
- **Washing lines** carry sheets, shirts, towels and trousers.
- **Shrubs** grow at the back corners of many houses, laurels and golden privet among them.
- **Flowers are their own colours** now, in window boxes and pots by the door too, where before they came out green.
- **Ivy** on old walls is a thick mat of leaves with ragged edges, where before it was striped clumps.
- The plants are built from the trees' photographed leaves. Apart from the hedges between gardens they're scenery: a shell or a passing truck flattens them, and they give no cover.
