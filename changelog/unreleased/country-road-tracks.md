- **Drawn by the ground itself**: gravel roads and supply tracks are part of the terrain now, lit and shaded like the meadow round them, so they sit in the land instead of on top of it.
- **Gravel roads**: traffic keeps to the middle of the road, pressing two smooth wheel paths into the fines; loose stone banks up between the wheels and out on the shoulders. The surface changes tone along the road where it was laid and patched, and potholes cluster in the wheel paths where drainage is worst.
- **Edges by height**: the grass comes back in tufts between the stones over a metre of spilled gravel and soil, then thickens into the meadow, instead of stopping at a line.
- **Supply tracks** are bare earth: their wheel ruts wander, widen and fade along the track, with a crown of grass between them in places and shoulders that roll off into the turf.
- **In the rain** water stands in the ruts and the potholes.
- **At the edge of town** the asphalt breaks up into the gravel in ragged bays, its crumbling rim packed with grit.
- **In the rain** streets go dark and glossy with standing water in the dips, the same across every junction.
- **Junctions** no longer show faint outlines or gutter lines where their paving meets the road.
- **Gutters** collect dead leaves against the kerb in some stretches.
- **Sidewalk joints** hold grit, and moss where they stay damp.
- **Car parks** are kerbed on their three open sides instead of a bare slab dropped on the grass.
- **Cracks in town asphalt** run along and across the streets, sealed with tar, instead of curly lines.

Wheel-rut lighting now follows the depressed surface: the sides shade into the rut, and the shoulders slope back into the meadow.
