- Jets and the Kajman have clear, smoked canopies with the cockpit inside: the seat and its headrest, the
  pilot's helmet and shoulders, the coaming and, in a fighter, the head-up display.
- Aircraft carry bright red and green position lights at their wingtips and red anti-collision beacons that
  you can pick out from above. Each jet carries its own number, rocket pods are loaded, jet nozzles are bare
  heat-tinted metal, and helicopter windscreens have wipers.
- Tank guns grow out of a pleated canvas bellows, strapped at the turret, its canvas faded differently from
  crew to crew. The muzzle end is bare, blast-burnt steel, the thermal sleeve shows its clamped joints and
  staggered seams, and the fume extractor its weld.
- Military truck and carrier wheels are pierced steel. Tracks show their bolt heads and pin ends and rest on
  their return rollers, sagging between them; some crews have mended a track with a run of new links, and now
  and then a road wheel is a replacement in another colour.
- Bolt heads and hatch hinges no longer glint from the battle camera, sights and vision blocks are coated
  glass, and autocannon muzzles are burnt black.
- Exhaust soot fades into the camouflage on more hulls. Slat cages are painted whole, frames and all, built in
  bolted panels and set closer; some armour modules are depot replacements in plain paint, and walkways read
  as gritty paint.
- The M35 and the FIA truck have cabs you can see into, ladder frames under them and, on the M35, an exhaust
  stack. The HEMTT's rolled cab roof shows, battery boxes are lidded bins, tarps are lashed down and drums
  come in mixed paint.
- Cars have moulded mirrors, and now and then a parked hatchback wears a bonnet from another car.
