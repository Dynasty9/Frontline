- Some rebel technicals and armed pickups bolt steel plate over their doors and bed walls, spares and jerrycans
  are roped on, and the Zamak's load carries tied grain sacks.
- Team marks are painted on in a pale service blue or brick, a chevron on one side's vehicles and a band on the
  other's, and gather dust with the hull instead of reading as dark holes. Wing marks are painted on the wing.
- Tyres stay dusty rubber in a dusty battle instead of taking the hull's colour. Recognition panels are duller
  and much rarer. Armoured windows, vision blocks and sights are
  thick dark glass instead of sky-blue tiles.
- The backs of tilted trucks are open on their benches, the Tempest's back flap is canvas, tilts are a canvas
  colour of their own with bows you can see from above, and loads of crates vary in their boxes and stencils.
- Helicopter rotors show as a soft shimmer that doesn't hide the airframe, round fuselages are smooth, drone
  propellers are shaped, and the Blackfoot's crew show through its canopy.
- Vans and tractors have clear cab glass with seats, dash and wheel inside. Technicals' camouflage is brushed
  on, running boards are black plastic, and the kart, quad bike, hatchback and SUV look more like the real
  things.
- Ships and boats fly slack cloth ensigns; the motorboat has a bimini, the inflatables Hypalon tubes and vinyl
  seats, and lines, life rings and liferafts are fitted properly.
- Roof hatches no longer carry a painted cross, engine decks read as louvres instead of stripes, gun boots are
  pleated canvas, the Ifrit has framed armoured glass, hinges and bins, the BTR's
  engine deck has its intakes and hatch, and the T-100's glacis carries a V of reactive armour. Exhausts are
  louvred outlets, side skirts are painted, logs are banded logs, and some crews have lost a fuel drum.
