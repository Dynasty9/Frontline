- Truck tilts are one canvas cover pulled over the hoops instead of rigid panels: it sags between the bows, is
  laced to the bed along its hem, and is sun-faded on the roof. Some crews roll a side curtain up over the
  benches, some have a patched panel, and the rear flaps are rolled or laced shut. Tarps over loads take the
  shape of the boxes under them.
- Bags bulge and show their seams; rucksacks have lids and front straps, duffels are gathered by a drawstring,
  and a crew's kit mixes cotton, nylon and rubberised bags. Bedrolls are pinched by their straps and show the
  wound roll at their ends.
- Webbing straps, nylon kit, vinyl and PVC, and rope each look their own. Ratchet straps wrap the crates they
  hold down, civilian loads are roped with a trucker's hitch, and gun boots are rubberised cloth tied with
  webbing.
- Jet cockpits have an ejection seat with its cushion and harness, a pilot with an oxygen mask, and side
  consoles. Truck cabs have vinyl seats, a steering wheel and a gear lever; cars have their dash and seats
  where they belong and a back seat, in seat cloth that varies from car to car.
- Helicopter cabin doors slide open on troop seats in the Ghost Hawk, Mohawk and Orca, and the Huron's crew
  door shows its red bench; now and then a door is shut.
- Some NATO and independent vehicles carry a fluorescent orange or pink air-recognition panel on the roof.
  Off-roaders wear vinyl spare-wheel covers, and some parked cars have bed covers, sun shades or a tarp-roped
  load.
- Car trim is plastic instead of wet paint, technicals and rebel trucks are sun-baked and worn, and car bonnets
  and roofs catch the sun as a soft glint instead of a white stripe. The SUV stands like an SUV, and steps that
  floated under doors are hung on brackets.
- Outboard motors are moulded, the trawler's nets stay inside the boat, the submersible's duct is smooth, life
  jackets lie in the motorboat, rotor heads no longer glint white, and the patrol ship's roofs read as deck.
