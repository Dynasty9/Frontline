- **A ruin's walls break off** along their brick courses or stones, stepping back a brick at a time: highest at
  the corners and between the windows, lowest over the windows, some bays knocked down further than others.
- **The break is the wall's own masonry**, in the same brick or stone as the wall under it. On a rendered house
  it's the brick under the render, and the render outside and the plaster inside have come away from it in a
  ragged edge.
- **Broken bricks and stones lie at the foot** of the walls, more where more came down, and the odd one lies loose
  on the break.
- **The scorch is where the roof burned:** charred joist ends in their pockets at the top of the walls that still
  reach the eaves, soot on the plaster under them.
- **Inside, patches of plaster have flaked away** across the ruined wall, exposing brick below the windows as well as along the break. Intact rooms keep their existing finish.
- The walls give the same cover as before.
