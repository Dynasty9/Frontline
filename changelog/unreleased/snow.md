- **Pick it** under Settings > Lighting & Look > Time of day & weather, in the menu's Time and weather row, or in photo mode. A random battle snows now and then, about one in twenty.
- **The light** is flat and cold: a weak sun with barely any shadow, a pale blue-grey sky, and haze closing in.
- **Snowflakes** drift down with the wind and flutter as they fall, staying put in the world as you pan through them.
- **The ground** never lies evenly. The wind strips it thin off the rises that face it and the ridges, scours long streaks along its line and carves the packed snow into low ridges, and drops it deep and smooth in the hollows, the gullies and the lee of the hills; slopes facing the sun thin first. Where it's thin the winter grass, tussocks and stones poke through in clumps, and where it's deep the open ground is clean. Under the trees it lies thinner, with a bare well of needles round every trunk. Shell holes and the earth they throw up show as dark mud and grey slush, and tracks leave dark lines through it. Dirt roads become slush lanes between white verges; town streets are ploughed, with low banks of snow along the kerbs and black, wet slush in the wheel lines, and the pavements, paths and the square are trodden grey along the ways people take, with paths worn through the gardens.
- **Everything standing in it** gathers snow on its upper faces: roofs, window ledges, fence tops, sandbags, camouflage nets, trench spoil, trees, bushes and grass, and walls stay bare but for a crust plastered on the side facing the wind. Trees hold it in lumps on their boughs. Under the trees the leaf litter is buried, and the ferns go winter brown with snow in clumps on their fronds.
- **Roofs** each hold their own. The wind loads the slope in its lee and scours the one facing it, and the sunny slope thins first. Steep sheet roofs shed most of it in streaks; flat roofs keep a full coat with drifts banked against the parapets. The snow curls over the eaves in a rounded lip that shows how thick it lies. Barns are cold and keep a thick coat; most houses are lived in and warm, so their roofs thaw round the chimney and over the rooms, the snow going thin, then to grey slush, then to bare tile dark and glossy with meltwater, with icicles hanging from the eaves.
- **Vehicles** keep snow only on their flat tops (the deck, turret roof, fenders and stowage), in patches of their own; it slides off the glacis and sides, and the warm engine deck stays bare and wet. A vehicle standing still gathers a full load, and driving or swinging its turret shakes most of it off, so a parked or wrecked tank is far whiter than one in the fight.
- **Drifts.** The wind heaps the snow against walls, houses, hedges, fences and wrecks: banked against the side away from the wind, rising to a rounded crest a little way out and tailing off a few metres beyond, tapering round the ends of the wall, deeper along some stretches than others, longer behind a tall wall or a hedge, with a smaller bank on the windward side standing off a scoop the wind scours at the wall's foot. The thin outer edge meets the ground instead of floating above it. The grass in a drift is buried; knock the wall down and its drift goes with it, even while nearby drifts are still being built. Where roofs overlap, the remaining roof keeps the ground sheltered after one is destroyed.
- Opening a snow battle no longer leaves an unexplained thaw patch beneath the starting camera.
- **Heat melts it.** A fire thaws the snow round it, the ground to bare, wet earth with a rim of grey slush, and the streets, yards and neighbouring roofs to wet stone and tile, and it melts the snow off whatever is burning: a roof, a wreck, a tree. A vehicle that stands with its engine running leaves a damp, dark patch under it, and the bare patch on its engine deck spreads the longer it runs. A gun that has fired sheds the snow off its barrel until it cools. Thawed ground stays bare after the fire is out and only whitens again slowly while the snow keeps falling.
- Snow that starts mid-battle settles over a few minutes and thaws slowly when it stops. The deep snow muffles the wind and the distant battle.
- It's looks only: nothing sees, moves or burns any differently in snow. It works in the browser too.
