- **Pick it** under Settings > Lighting & Look > Time of day & weather, in the menu's Time and weather row, or in photo mode. A random battle snows now and then, about one in twenty.
- **The light** is flat and cold: a weak sun with barely any shadow, a pale blue-grey sky, and haze closing in.
- **Snowflakes** drift down with the wind and flutter as they fall, staying put in the world as you pan through them.
- **The ground** lies white on the flat and stays dark on steep slopes. Shell holes and the earth they throw up show as dark mud and grey slush, and tracks leave dark lines through it. Dirt roads become slush lanes between white verges; town streets keep black wheel paths and heaped white gutters.
- **Everything standing in it** gathers snow on its upper faces: roofs, window ledges, sandbags, camouflage nets, trench spoil, trees, bushes and grass. It never lies evenly: the wind scours streaks and open rises thin so the grass shows through, one roof holds more than the next, and trees hold it in lumps on their boughs. Under the trees the leaf litter is buried, and the ferns go winter brown with snow in clumps on their fronds.
- **Vehicles** keep snow only on their flat tops (the deck, turret roof, fenders and stowage), in patches of their own; it slides off the glacis and sides, and the warm engine deck stays bare and wet. A vehicle standing still gathers a full load, and driving or swinging its turret shakes most of it off, so a parked or wrecked tank is far whiter than one in the fight.
- **Drifts.** The wind heaps the snow against walls, houses, hedges, fences and wrecks: deepest against the side away from the wind and thinning out a few metres beyond, longer behind a tall wall or a hedge, with a smaller bank on the windward side. The grass in a drift is buried; knock the wall down and its drift goes with it, even while nearby drifts are still being built. Where roofs overlap, the remaining roof keeps the ground sheltered after one is destroyed.
- **Heat melts it.** A fire thaws the snow round it, the ground to bare, wet earth with a rim of grey slush, and the streets, yards and neighbouring roofs to wet stone and tile, and it melts the snow off whatever is burning: a roof, a wreck, a tree. A vehicle that stands with its engine running leaves a damp, dark patch under it, and the bare patch on its engine deck spreads the longer it runs. A gun that has fired sheds the snow off its barrel until it cools. Thawed ground stays bare after the fire is out and only whitens again slowly while the snow keeps falling.
- Snow that starts mid-battle settles over a few minutes and thaws slowly when it stops. The deep snow muffles the wind and the distant battle.
- It's looks only: nothing sees, moves or burns any differently in snow. It works in the browser too.
