- Tank guns wear a pleated canvas boot where they leave the turret. On the Abrams and the T-55 it rises and
  falls with the gun.
- Crews no longer dress their vehicles in cut branches or throw camouflage nets over the load: a vehicle
  carries the kit it's issued. Nets that stow rolled and strapped on the roof stay.
- Helicopter engine fairings sit down on the fuselage instead of hovering over it, and the Mohawk's and Ghost
  Hawk's cowlings are rounded. The Taru's pod roof has panels and vents.
- Jets carry a small exhaust glow instead of a big orange lamp. Their canopies sit in frames, and ailerons and
  flaps stand out from the wing. Drone wings show their panels.
- The Slammer's turret has the Merkava's arrowhead nose. The Rhino's turret sides carry bolted armour.
- Weapon stations, armour bolts, rails and machine guns no longer show bright metal from above. Dark steel
  reads as steel, and the blue ID panels are matte.
- More hulls carry spare track links, and tactical numbers are bigger.
- Road wheels have rounded rubber, and truck tyres carry their maker's lettering. Truck cab roofs are rounded,
  with rain gutters.
- The water scooter and outboard motors are moulded rather than faceted. Speedboats carry hull numbers. Rust
  and soot on the ships fade into the paint.
